Golden hour

You paid a scammer. Here's what to do, in order.

Breathe. This happens to careful people every day. The sooner it's reported, the better the chance the bank can hold the money before it's withdrawn.

Time since you paid

--:--

  1. Call 1930

    The national cyber-crime helpline. Tell them the amount, the time and the UTR number from your payment. They can alert the banks involved to hold the money.

    Call 1930 now

  2. Call your bank

    Use the number on the back of your card or in your bank's official app, never one from a search result or from the caller. Ask them to block UPI and net banking and to raise a fraud dispute for this payment.

  3. Report at cybercrime.gov.in

    Choose Report Financial Fraud. You can paste the complaint we draft below. Write down the acknowledgement number they give you; the bank will ask for it.

    Open cybercrime.gov.in

  4. Lock things down

    Change your UPI PIN and net-banking password. If they made you install AnyDesk or another app, uninstall it now. If you shared an OTP, tell the bank what it was for.

  5. Keep the evidence

    Don't delete the chat or the call log. Screenshot the chat, the caller's number, the payment and the UPI ID they used.

  6. Watch for the second scam

    Anyone who contacts you promising to "recover" the money for a fee is also a scammer, even if they say they're police, a lawyer or the bank.

  7. Tell someone you trust

    You don't have to handle this alone. These are organised gangs who do this all day; being fooled once isn't a reflection on you.
